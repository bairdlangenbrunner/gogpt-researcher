# Russia

Large, fragmented fleet spread across many holding companies and regional
"scheme and programme" planning documents; the single most important source
is the national SO UPS scheme-and-programme document, supplemented heavily by
company-level and regional-government sources — most in Russian.

## Regulators & official sources

- **System Operator of the United Power System of Russia (SO UPS)** —
  publishes the **most important and comprehensive document for oil and gas
  plants**: the "Scheme and programme for development of the electric power
  systems of Russia" ("Схема и программа развития электроэнергетических
  систем России" / "SiPR"), at
  [so-ups.ru/future-planning/sipr-ees](https://www.so-ups.ru/future-planning/sipr-ees/).
  It comprises:
  1. A Ministry of Energy order approving the scheme/programme for a given
     multi-year window — a summary document covering planned construction,
     retirement, and upgrades.
  2. **Supporting materials (by region)** ("Обосновывающие материалы") — the
     detailed document: operating and in-development coal/gas/oil plants by
     region, unit counts, capacity, fuel, and start/retirement years. As of
     March 2026, the most recent supporting materials publicly available
     covered 2025-2030; the 2026-2031 edition had moved to a
     [new portal](https://ppr.so-ups.ru/vis/sipr/) that was not accessible at
     that time — check whether that portal is now reachable.
  3. **Implementation reports** ("Отчеты о реализации СиПР ЭЭС России") — as
     of the source doc, no reports had yet been posted, but SO UPS's stated
     intent is to store all future progress reports at
     [so-ups.ru/future-planning/sipr-ees/reports](https://www.so-ups.ru/future-planning/sipr-ees/reports/).
  - SO UPS also publishes region/OES ("Объединенная энергосистема" / United
    Energy System)-level pages with "largest generating facilities" lists,
    e.g. [OES East](https://www.so-ups.ru/functioning/ees/oes-east/), and
    profiles of individual significant facilities (example:
    [Arkhangelsk THC page](https://www.so-ups.ru/odu-northwest/odu-northwest-zone/znachimye-ehnergoobekty/arkhangelskaja-tehc/)).
  - Numerous **regional energy ministries** publish their own
    scheme-and-programme documents (same "SiPR" concept, at the regional
    level) — e.g. Moscow Region, Primorsky Territory, Volgograd Region,
    Smolensk Region, Samara Region, Kamchatka Territory. These are useful
    when a plant of interest falls in a specific region and the national SiPR
    supporting materials are unavailable or out of date for that region.
- **Ministry of Energy of Russia (Minenergo)** — [minenergo.gov.ru/en](https://minenergo.gov.ru/en) —
  periodic commissioning/capacity statistics.

## Research tips

- Russian-language fluency (or at minimum comfort working through machine
  translation) is close to a requirement for this country — the great
  majority of useful sources, including the core SO UPS SiPR documents and
  most operator pages, are Russian-only or Russian-primary.
- When the national SiPR supporting materials are unavailable or out of date
  for a region of interest, check whether that region's own energy ministry
  publishes its own scheme-and-programme document — several examples are
  listed above.
- Location/subnational-unit note: Moscow and Saint Petersburg are federal
  cities and do **not** belong to Moscow Oblast / Leningrad Oblast
  respectively — when recording subnational unit, use "Moscow" or "Saint
  Petersburg" directly, and take care not to conflate "Moscow" (the city)
  with "Moscow Oblast" (the surrounding region).

## Gotchas

- **City vs. oblast confusion**: Moscow and Saint Petersburg are standalone
  federal subjects, equal in status to the surrounding oblasts — don't record
  a Moscow-city plant's subnational unit as "Moscow Oblast" or a
  Saint-Petersburg plant as "Leningrad Oblast."
- The national-level SiPR "supporting materials" (the actually detailed,
  plant-level document) can lag a full planning cycle behind the summary
  order — as of March 2026 the most current publicly accessible supporting
  materials were for 2025-2030, one cycle behind the 2026-2031 order, because
  the newer edition had moved to an inaccessible portal. Always check which
  cycle's supporting materials you're actually looking at.
- Given the number of holding companies (Gazprom Energoholding alone has
  several major subsidiaries), a given plant's ownership may be documented
  inconsistently across a parent company's page vs. a subsidiary's own page —
  prefer the most specific (operating subsidiary) page when they conflict.
