## State context

- No independent system operator and no capacity auction. Each utility runs its own balancing area and plans its own supply. The Florida Reliability Coordinating Council's regional entity role moved to SERC in 2019.
- Ten-Year Site Plans are the main planning source. Ten utilities file every April 1 with the Public Service Commission: Florida Power and Light, Duke Energy Florida, Tampa Electric, the Florida Municipal Power Agency, Gainesville Regional Utilities, JEA, Lakeland Electric, the Orlando Utilities Commission, Seminole Electric Cooperative and the City of Tallahassee. Plans are posted at floridapsc.com/ten-year-site-plans (the page is a shell for the fetch tool; the PDFs are at floridapsc.com/pscfiles/website-files/PDF/Utilities/Electricgas/TenYearSitePlans/<year>/<Utility>.pdf and open with curl). The commission's "suitable" finding is a planning review, not approval to build. Retirement dates in a plan are targets and are marked approximate.
- Need determinations: the Power Plant Siting Act applies at 75 MW and above (Section 403.503). The commission decides need under Section 403.519 in dockets ending "-EM" (municipal or cooperative) or "-EI" (investor-owned). A 50 to 74 MW project needs no need case, so its first public sign is often an air permit.
- Filings open by document number at floridapsc.com/library/filings/<year>/<number>/<number>.pdf. Site certification is run by the Department of Environmental Protection's Siting Coordination Office (floridadep.gov/siting); air permits are in the Air Permit Documents Search (fldep.dep.state.fl.us/air/emission/apds).
- Florida Power and Light absorbed Gulf Power on January 1, 2021; its 2026 plan still labels the northwest plants "FPL NWFL" (Gulf Clean Energy Center, Lansing Smith, Pea Ridge). Duke Energy Florida was Progress Energy Florida.
- Rate cases: Florida Power and Light's four-year settlement was approved November 20, 2025 (2026 through 2029); Duke's was approved August 22, 2024 (2025 through 2027). Neither commits to new gas plants.

## Recent events 2024 to 2026

- JEA: the commission found need in August 2026 (Docket 20260087-EM) for a 675 MW combined cycle unit on the former St. Johns River Power Park site, $1.57 billion, planned for 2031. GEM has it as JEA Power Island power station, 700 MW, pre-construction. The possible-updates sheet says it should be combined with St. Johns River Power Park.
- Florida Power and Light 2026 plan: 3,276 MW of new combustion turbines at Martin from 2032 to 2035 ("2032 4x0 Martin CTs" 932 MW, same in 2033 and 2034, "2035 2x0 Martin CTs" 596 MW; field construction starts 2028 and 2029); Vandolah purchase (660 to 672 MW, FERC approval December 2025, serving the company from June 1, 2027); Gulf Clean Energy Center units 4 and 5 retire at the end of 2029; Lansing Smith CT 3A (40 MW, oil) retires in the fourth quarter of 2027; Manatee unit 3 and Martin unit 3 upgrades in 2027 and 2028.
- Duke Energy Florida 2025 plan: four combustion turbines in 2033 and 2034 (site not named); Bayboro units 1 to 4 retire September 2026; Bartow peakers 1 and 3 and DeBary peakers 2 to 6 retire June 2027; Intercession City 1 to 6, Suwannee River 1 to 3 and Bartow peakers 2 and 4 retire June 2034; close to 400 MW of combined cycle efficiency gains.
- Tampa Electric: the coal gasification side of Polk unit 1 retired at the end of 2024 and the combustion turbine is being upgraded; Polk unit 2 gets two 59.5 MW enhancements (November 2026, June 2027); the board approved a Big Bend unit 4 modernization to combined cycle after the April 2026 plan was filed, with a schedule promised in the 2027 plan.
- Orlando Utilities Commission 2025 plan: Stanton unit 1 goes to extended cold shutdown by the end of May 2026 instead of converting to gas; Stanton unit 2 is to run on gas only. Osceola peakers were bought in 2021; Indian River turbines A to D remain.
- Florida Municipal Power Agency: closed on a combined cycle plant at the start of 2026 for its All-Requirements Project (plant not named in the pages read).
- Gainesville 2026 plan: Deerhaven turbines 1 and 2 and steam units 1 and 2 tentatively retire in 2031, 2032 and 2036, dates may slip.

## Gotchas

- Many Florida peakers are small units of 40 to 75 MW grouped by the dozen (Intercession City 14, DeBary 9, Bartow 4). Each GEM row is its own unit, several are oil only, and the same plant can have units retiring in 2026, 2027 and 2034.
- Unsited future units: Florida Power and Light names plan lines by year and site ("2032 4x0 Martin CTs") and lists 94 preferred and 38 potential sites. A potential site is a land holding, not a project.
- Site plans change fast. Big Bend unit 4 is missing from the 2026 plan because the board approved it afterward. Treat a plan's retirement year as a target.
- Coal to gas records: Gulf Clean Energy Center converted in 2020; Deerhaven unit 2 and the Stanton units carry "timepoint 2" rows. Stanton unit 1 is cold shutdown, not conversion. Big Bend units are being rebuilt as combined cycle, so one unit can be a coal boiler, a gas boiler and a combined cycle block over its life.
- Municipal utilities own shares of each other's plants (the Orlando Utilities Commission holds 68.6 percent of Stanton unit 1). Record owners from the plan's ownership notes.
- The three Seminole IRP placeholders and the Duke IRP placeholder share one coordinate pair in GEM (the duplicate scan flags them). They are different utilities' plans, not duplicates, but the coordinates are placeholders.
- C.D. McIntosh and Larsen Memorial (Lakeland) and Hardee and Midulla are neighbors with separate EIA plant codes, not duplicates.
