# Scope-wide tasks

## When a project GEM lacks is added (Baird 2026-10-23)

Write a new plant record (shards/_state.json, newplants) or a new unit at a tracked plant (newunits) as soon as ONE verified public source names a gas or oil generating project, places it in this scope, and gives a capacity at the threshold (20 MW; engine and aeroderivative sets summed), a unit count with a plant total, or no capacity at all. The manual adds a plant whose capacity cannot be found with the cell blank and removes it later if it proves small. A missing start year, owner, turbine model or exact point never holds an add back: leave the field blank and say in researcher_notes what was not found.

Research the owner on every add all the same, and stage it (Baird 2026-10-29). The owner is the company the documents name as building or owning the project: the developer, the data center company, the utility or a project company. A legal entity name is not needed. A landowner who only filed a zoning petition is not the owner, nor is a buyer of the power. Run entity_lookup.py --pg and use the existing entity's exact name. Leave the owner blank only when no source names any company behind the project.

The manual's status ladder decides the status, never the add. A long-range planning document, a grid-operator interconnection queue entry or a press announcement is announced. A permit application, land, financing, a prior-approval docket or a signed interconnection agreement is pre-construction. A project found already cancelled, shelved or retired since 2020 is an add with that status. A queue entry under a shell company's name is still an add at announced, named after the queue entry or its site; the database already holds such plants.

A watch item (monitor, new to the tracker) is only for these cases, and names its case in watch_case: no_generation (a campus, site or program with no generating unit named yet), under_threshold (every unit under the bar and not an engine or aeroderivative set that sums to it), drive_or_storage (generator or mechanical drive, or thermal or storage, unresolved), duplicate_check (it may be a unit GEM already has; prefer a qa question), not_committed (a plan studies it only as an option), other_scope (another state or country; the evidence file lists it as a handoff), unverified (nothing passed the verifier yet). Anything else is an add.

A data center is never a watch case for being a data center or for being backup power. A gas or diesel plant at a data center, routine power or a backup engine fleet, is an add when the summed electrical nameplate of its generators reaches the threshold (20 MW), with the capacity cell blank when no figure can be found. Name it "<Operator> <Place> Data Center power station", name the data center in Project Links, and set Backup Power yes at unit level only on backup engine units (briefs/_data_centers.md lists the sites when a permit harvest exists).

Every new record uses exact export headers in fields and keys refs by Data Source column with a link for every filled field; a grid-operator spreadsheet is cited by the download address with the matching row quoted in researcher_notes.

- add (stage it as a new plant in the newplants lane)
- below_threshold (under the add threshold, with the capacity evidence)
- out_of_scope (not a combustion plant GOGPT tracks, with the evidence)
- already_in_gem (it sits under another name; give the GEM plant ID)

## Statewide sources to search

Search every source below. For each, record an entry in batches/germany/coverage.json under `sources`, keyed by the id: `{"status": "searched", "how": "the script or pages read", "found": "what turned up, or that nothing did"}`. A source you could not read is `blocked`, with an `access` note that says what fetch.py returned and what the Wayback lookup found. Never write "not searched" or "skipped". A search form, a script or a paged table is never a reason to stop. The permit record system (marked below) is searched by name for every GEM plant in the scope and for every project or data center lead, not only browsed: a public notice feed lists only the permits that went to notice, and a small construction permit never does. "No permit found" for a named site is only true once that system was searched for the name, and the note says so. The local planning records (marked below) are opened for every lead and every in-development plant: the city, county or township agendas, staff reports, packets, minutes, rezonings, plats, conditional or special use permits, building permits, development agreements and tax abatements, and any permit application the developer posts. They name generator fleets and sites long before a state permit is filed.

- uvp-verbund [permit record system]: UVP-Verbund, the federal and state register of environmental assessment and immission-control permit procedures (https://www.uvp-verbund.de/). Run or reuse scopes/germany/harvest_permits.py (scopes/germany/work/permits/), which searches the register for every GEM plant and every lead by plant name, operator and town, and reads each procedure page (trefferanzeige?docuuid=<ID>) and its documents: the application notice, the short description with unit type, maker, model and electrical and thermal MW, the hearing dates and the approval. The state front ends (uvp.niedersachsen.de, www.uvp.sachsen.de and the like) are the same server; fetch.py handles the 429 throttle. A procedure that is not in the register can still exist at a state authority, so search those too (next entry).
- state-permit-portals [public notice feed]: State permit authority notice pages (Bavaria, Hesse, North Rhine-Westphalia, Baden-Wuerttemberg, Saxony, Saxony-Anhalt) (https://www.brd.nrw.de/). Read the public notice and hearing pages of the district governments and state offices that publish outside or alongside the register: the Bavarian Regierungen, rp-darmstadt.hessen.de and the other Hessian Regierungspraesidien, rp.baden-wuerttemberg.de, lds.sachsen.de, lvwa.sachsen-anhalt.de and the Bezirksregierungen of North Rhine-Westphalia, for every gas or oil plant application, draft approval and approval of 20 MW or more, data centers and captive plants included.
