## State context

- Two grid operators. PJM Interconnection covers the ComEd territory in northern Illinois (Chicago, Joliet, Aurora, Rockford, University Park, Dixon). The Midcontinent Independent System Operator (MISO) covers the Ameren Illinois territory in central and southern Illinois. Elwood units 5 to 7 are now owned by Dairyland Power Cooperative and serve MISO members, so one site near Joliet now straddles the line.
- Retirement notices live on PJM's generator deactivation page (pjm.com/planning/service-requests/gen-deactivations/generator-deactivation-notices). PJM posts the owner's letter and its own reply, and later any withdrawal letter. Illinois entries in 2024 to 2026: Elwood (several letters, May 2024 to May 2026), the NRG gas and oil fleet (July 2026), Midwest Generation (July 2026), and the Fisk and Waukegan peakers (February 2025).
- The Climate and Equitable Jobs Act of 2021 (Public Act 102-0662, rules in Section 9.15 of the Illinois Environmental Protection Act) sets emissions deadlines for private gas units over 25 MW: January 1, 2030 for high-emitting units in or near environmental justice areas, 2035 for cleaner units in those areas, 2040 for the rest of the private fleet, and 2045 for every remaining unit including municipal, cooperative and cogeneration units. The deadline depends on each unit's emission rates and location, so the statute names no plants, and no official list by deadline year exists. A grid operator can keep a unit running past its deadline for reliability.
- That reliability exception is in use. On July 2, 2026 PJM wrote to NRG that its Illinois gas and oil units (Aurora, University Park North and South, Rockford, about 2,376 MW) must deactivate by December 31, 2029 under the law, and asked NRG to keep them running through May 31, 2031, citing the 2027/2028 capacity shortfall. A matching letter covers Midwest Generation's Fisk and Waukegan oil peakers and the Powerton coal units. Whether NRG agreed is not confirmed.
- The Clean and Reliable Grid Affordability Act (Public Act 104-0458, signed January 8, 2026, in force June 1, 2026) adds 3 GW of storage by 2030, lifts the nuclear ban, creates an integrated resource planning process, and tightens standards for data center backup generators from December 1, 2026. It does not change the gas closure deadlines and does not ban new gas plants.
- Regulators and sources: the Illinois Commerce Commission (icc.illinois.gov, e-Docket for filings; most gas plants are merchant and do not file there), the Illinois Environmental Protection Agency Bureau of Air (air permits and public notices at epa.illinois.gov/topics/forms/air-permits.html and epa.illinois.gov/public-notices/boa-notices.html; a new plant needs a construction permit, then a Title V operating permit), the Illinois Power Agency (renewables and storage procurement only), PJM's queue (a large XML file at pjm.com/pub/planning/downloads/xml/PlanningQueues.xml), MISO's queue status tool, and the EIA-860 and EIA-860M tables.
- PJM's July 2025 capacity auction set a record $329.17 per megawatt-day in the ComEd zone for 2026/2027. High prices make owners less likely to retire plants and more likely to sell them.

## Gotchas

- Statutory deadlines are not retirement dates. Do not stage a planned retirement year from the 2021 law alone; use the owner's deactivation letter, and look for a withdrawal letter before marking a unit retired.
- Elwood is one GEM plant with nine units and now has two owners on two grids. Check each unit's owner and retirement date separately.
- PJM letters get revised. Elwood had three retirement dates in two years. Record only the latest letter.
- Neighbor names: Elwood is near Joliet, but Joliet 9 and Joliet 29 are separate retired plants. University Park North and South are two GEM plants that PJM lists separately. Fisk and Waukegan oil peakers (units 31 to 34 and 31 to 32) are not in GEM; only the coal units are.
- Cleanview lists interconnection points ("Austin Substation 345kV Bus") as plants. They are grid connection points, not plants.
- Cogeneration and public (municipal, cooperative) units run on the 2045 clock. Elwood units 5 to 7 are cooperative-owned now, but the statute's "public unit" test needs the whole unit to be publicly owned.
- Lincoln Land's permit talks about hydrogen blends. Hydrogen columns are not researched.
