## State context

- Almost all of Kansas is in the Southwest Power Pool (SPP). Projects in the queue are named GEN-year-number. Kansas Gas and Electric is now called Evergy Kansas South, and Westar is Evergy Kansas Central.
- The utility regulator is the Kansas Corporation Commission (KCC). Dockets are numbered like 25-EKCE-207-RTS, with the utility code in the middle. A plant needs a siting permit for a generator tie line (a docket ending in STG), and cost recovery orders for new plants come from the KCC. The press release page is kcc.ks.gov.
- Air construction permits come from the Kansas Department of Health and Environment (KDHE) Bureau of Air. Proposed permits are posted for public comment on the Bureau's public notice page and in the Kansas Register. The state's permit system is the Kansas Environmental Information Management System (KEIMS) at keims.kdhe.ks.gov. It is a script-driven site, so `harvest_permits_kansas.py` reads it ahead of a sweep. It is never a reason to skip a source. Hosts are in `docs/reference/site_access.md`.
- Evergy's two new combined cycle plants are Viola (Sumner County, near the Viola substation, expected January 2029) and McNew (Reno County, next to Hutchinson, expected January 2030). Each is about 705 to 710 MW, owned half by Evergy Kansas Central and half by Evergy Missouri West. The KCC approved cost recovery in July 2025 and the Missouri commission issued its order on July 31, 2025. Evergy's first quarter 2026 report says McNew is being built under an engineering contract with Kiewit, with Mitsubishi supplying the power equipment. A Reno County generator tie line siting application was filed on November 14, 2025 (docket 26-EKCE-0148-STG).
- Lawrence Energy Center and Jeffrey Energy Center are coal plants whose planned gas units sit in GEM as "timepoint 2" announced units. Retirement dates for both coal sites have slipped repeatedly. A Lawrence conversion has been reported both as filed with the KCC and as scrapped, and the dates of those reports were not confirmed. Read the current Evergy plan before trusting any date.
- Five placeholder plants, "Evergy Kansas Central 2025 IRP power station 1 to 5", hold gas capacity from Evergy Kansas Central's May 2025 plan update that has no site. The plan lists 355 MW combined cycle in 2029 and 2030, 710 MW combined cycle in 2031 and 2033, and gas turbines later. Viola and McNew are the 2029 and 2030 blocks, so check whether a placeholder duplicates them. The placeholders carry map points that were not set from any document. They are not real sites.
- Walnut Energy Center in Winfield (Cowley County) belongs to the Kansas Power Pool. The EIA file lists the site as East 12th Street, plant 7013. A different 301 MW plant of the same name, in California, is not this plant.
- Kansas City Board of Public Utilities (BPU) owns Kaw, Nearman Creek and Quindaro. The EIA file shows the three Kaw steam units retired in 2024 while GEM says mothballed, and shows Quindaro units 1 and 2 retired in 2024 while GEM says 2019.

## Watch items to raise with the reviewer

- Evergy Metro's plan update lists 440 MW of gas turbines in 2031 and 355 MW combined cycle blocks from 2032 on. The tab says they may be in Missouri or Kansas.
- Whether the Kansas City BPU plan adds any gas is open. Its 2024 plan only keeps gas as an option.

## Gotchas

- Coal-to-gas conversions are separate units named "<name>, timepoint 2", with the coal unit as timepoint 1. Jeffrey unit 2 and Lawrence units 4 and 5 timepoint 2 show as announced in GEM, while the EIA table lists the coal units as operating. Check that the status belongs to the gas conversion and not the coal unit.
- The Lawrence Energy Center Other IDs box lists the code rec_d4f1u5oq7easr8ra1fc0 once as EIA and once as EIP. It is an EIP record code, so the EIA label is a data entry error to remove by hand.
- Hutchinson GT4 is 70 MW in GEM and 129 MW nameplate in the EIA table.
