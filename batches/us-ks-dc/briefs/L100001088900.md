## State context

- Almost all of Kansas is in the Southwest Power Pool, called SPP. Projects in its queue are named GEN-year-number. Kansas Gas and Electric is now called Evergy Kansas South, and Westar is Evergy Kansas Central.
- The utility regulator is the Kansas Corporation Commission, called the KCC. Dockets are numbered like 25-EKCE-207-RTS, with the utility code in the middle. A generator tie line needs a siting permit, and that docket ends in STG. Cost recovery orders for new plants also come from the KCC. Its press release page is kcc.ks.gov.
- Air construction permits come from the Kansas Department of Health and Environment, called KDHE, Bureau of Air. Proposed permits are posted for public comment on the Bureau's public notice page and in the Kansas Register. The state's permit system is the Kansas Environmental Information Management System, called KEIMS, at keims.kdhe.ks.gov. It is a script-driven site, so `harvest_permits_kansas.py` reads it ahead of a sweep. It is never a reason to skip a source. Hosts are in `docs/reference/site_access.md`.
- Evergy's two new combined cycle plants are Viola and McNew. Viola is in Sumner County, near the Viola substation, expected in January 2029. McNew is in Reno County, next to Hutchinson, expected in January 2030. Each is about 705 to 710 MW, owned half by Evergy Kansas Central and half by Evergy Missouri West. The KCC approved cost recovery in July 2025 and the Missouri commission issued its order on July 31, 2025. Evergy's first quarter 2026 report says McNew is being built under an engineering contract with Kiewit, with Mitsubishi supplying the power equipment. KCC docket 25-EKCE-207-PRE says Kansas Central owns 355 MW at each plant, with commercial operation January 1, 2029 at Viola and January 1, 2030 at McNew. Docket 26-EKCE-0148-STG is a 345 kV transmission line siting docket, not a generator tie. Evergy renamed Viola the Chisholm Trail Energy Center and the plant is in Conway Springs. Viola is moving to construction on Evergy's release and the Evergy Missouri filing; the EIA table says 765 MW, Evergy's sources say about 710 MW.
- Lawrence Energy Center and Jeffrey Energy Center are coal plants whose planned gas units sit in GEM as "timepoint 2" announced units. Retirement dates for both coal sites have slipped repeatedly. A Lawrence conversion has been reported both as filed with the KCC and as scrapped, and the dates of those reports were not confirmed. Read the current Evergy plan before trusting any date.
- Five placeholder plants named "Evergy Kansas Central 2025 IRP power station 1" to "5" hold gas capacity from Evergy Kansas Central's May 2025 plan update that has no site. The plan lists 355 MW combined cycle in 2029 and 2030, 710 MW combined cycle in 2031 and 2033, and gas turbines later. Viola and McNew are the 2029 and 2030 blocks, so check whether a placeholder duplicates them. The placeholders carry map points that were not set from any document. They are not real sites, so the location step must not move them. Placeholder 1 matches the EIP facility Crooked Creek Energy Center (KDHE permit 2050087, Benedict, Wilson County, one block of about 705 MW), but Evergy's 2026 plan puts its 2031 710 MW block at Buchanan Bluffs in Missouri, so routing is a reviewer call.
- Walnut Energy Center is in Winfield, Cowley County, and belongs to the Kansas Power Pool. The EIA file lists the site as East 12th Street, plant 7013. A different 301 MW plant of the same name, in California, is not this plant.
- Kansas City Board of Public Utilities, called BPU, owns Kaw, Nearman Creek and Quindaro. The EIA file shows the three Kaw steam units retired in 2024 while GEM says mothballed. It shows Quindaro units 1 and 2 retired in 2024 while GEM says 2019.
