## State context

- Grid operator: the Southwest Power Pool (SPP) for the whole state, the panhandle included (Xcel's Southwestern Public Service territory is SPP too). SPP's active generator interconnection queue, including the Expedited Resource Adequacy Study (ERAS) rows, is an open CSV read by `pull_queues.py --state OK`. ERAS rows are utility-sponsored fast-track thermal projects: in 2025 OG&E, Public Service Company of Oklahoma (PSO, an AEP company) and Grand River Dam Authority each sponsored one or more.
- Regulators: the Oklahoma Corporation Commission (OCC) Public Utility Division hears certificates of public convenience and necessity, preapproval of generation, rate cases and integrated resource plan filings for OG&E and PSO. Cases filed since 2022-03-21 live in the OCC Electronic Case Filing system (case numbers like PUD2026-000073), which needs a sign-in to search; the daily Public Utility Division docket PDFs on oklahoma.gov are the anonymous way in. Cooperatives (Western Farmers Electric Cooperative, Associated Electric Cooperative, Grand River Dam Authority, Oklahoma Municipal Power Authority, Peoples Electric Cooperative) are not rate-regulated by the OCC and do not file for preapproval; their plants show up in SPP, in air permits and in their own annual reports.
- Air permits: the Oklahoma Department of Environmental Quality (ODEQ) Air Quality Division. The old deq.ok.gov pages are gone (301 to oklahoma.gov/deq). Every permit action in process sits in the anonymous "Permits for Public Review" table at applications.deq.ok.gov, and the documents behind it (applications, draft permits, notices, back to 2016) come from an anonymous OnBase document API. Issued permits drop off the table, but three more anonymous sources keep the history: the public notice store at applications.deq.ok.gov/permitspublic (numbered PDFs of every notice, draft permit and permit memo, back to about 2020), four CSV permit lists on oklahoma.gov (in active review, issued in the past year, open for petition, and every major source PSD permit since 2001) and one public comment PDF per permit on oklahoma.gov. ## Gotchas

- The ODEQ permit table host has a certificate that fails verification: fetch it with verification off (`fetch.py` records `insecure_tls`). Match company and facility names case-insensitively; the table mixes "Oklahoma" and "OKLAHOMA".
- OnBase document addresses are citable, but the document IDs in them change with each search; cite the address the harvest saved and read it soon.
- Only the OCC daily docket files whose names end in `-pud` matter; the others are oil and gas, transportation and consumer dockets.
- The OCC Laserfiche WebLink answers anonymously: electronic filings are cited at ElectronicFile.aspx and imaged orders at DocView.aspx, read through the `occ_weblink` rule in `fetch.py`.
- OnBase search results carry a document type and a date but not the facility name. Always query by permit number or by the table's object id, so every PDF is tied to the permit it belongs to.
- Mustang Energy Center and Oneta return zero documents from the ODEQ document store because their permits predate it. The ODEQ public notice store has their permit memos: notice 4458 for Mustang (the 2018 renewal memo, which describes the seven gas turbines that replaced the old boilers) and notice 8041 for Oneta (the 2021 memo). Cite those.
- The ODEQ notice store has no search. Its PDFs are numbered (about 7916 to 9270 dense in 2026, sparse below); the harvester probes every number and keeps a text copy of each. The permit memo's SUBJECT block gives permit number, company, facility, coordinates and address.
- The oklahoma.gov permit lists are plain CSV files. Three are Latin-1 text; the PSD list is UTF-8 with a byte order mark. The county column sometimes holds the permit class (major, minor, syn-min) instead of a county.
- The public comment PDF folder on oklahoma.gov cannot be listed and its file names are case-sensitive (title case works). A miss there proves nothing.
- A Thunderbird Power Plant permit from 2001 in Cleveland County and the Horseshoe Energy Project in Lincoln County are not the Thunderbird data center at Enid or OG&E's Horseshoe Lake.
- The OCC legacy case search only holds cases before 2022 and takes numeric case numbers. Date searches return nothing. The current system's API answers 401 without a token.
- OGE Energy's investor site needs the browser-fingerprint route; oge.com's old IRP paths are 404. The OCC copy of the 2025 IRP needs nothing special.
- The SPP CSV has a "Last Updated On" line before its header; `pull_queues.py` skips it. The SPP "Nearest Town or County" column is often a substation name ("AES 5", "Muskoge7"), not a place.
- Most scripts write `work/<thing>_us-oklahoma.*`; `worklist.py` and `location_candidates.py` write `work/<thing>_us-ok.*`.
- The export spells the county column values inconsistently and leaves many blank; the Update SOP's county convention applies to every staged location edit.
